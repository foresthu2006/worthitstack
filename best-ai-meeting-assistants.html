AI meeting assistants are one of the few AI categories with a clear, proven ROI: they join your calls, transcribe everything, and hand you summaries with action items — no more "wait, who was supposed to do that?" The category has matured fast, and the tools now differ less on transcription quality (all good) and more on workflow fit: does it join as a visible bot or record silently? Does it push notes to your CRM? Does the free tier actually cover a real team?

One non-negotiable before the list: recording consent. Many jurisdictions require all parties' consent to record, and most companies require disclosure. Every tool here announces itself or shows a recording indicator — tell attendees you're recording regardless. Our verdicts come from documented features, pricing, and aggregated review sentiment, not hands-on lab tests.

Deep dive: each assistant

1. Otter.ai — best all-rounder

Otter is the category's default pick: real-time transcription, AI summaries, action-item extraction, and integrations with Zoom, Meet, and Teams. Its differentiators are the mobile app and in-person recording mode — it handles hallway conversations and conference rooms, not just video calls. Reported 2026 pricing: a free tier (300 minutes/month) and Pro at ~$16.99/month monthly or ~$8.33/month on annual billing. Reviewers consistently rate transcription accuracy among the best, and the free tier is genuinely usable for light users. Weaknesses: the meeting bot announces itself like everyone else's, and heavy users hit per-user pricing that scales with team size.

2. Fireflies.ai — best for sales and CRM workflows

Fireflies positions itself as the meeting assistant for revenue teams: beyond transcription and summaries, it offers deal intelligence (talk ratios, sentiment, competitor mentions), 100+ integrations, and workflow automation that pushes notes and tasks into HubSpot, Salesforce, Asana, and Slack. If your meetings are sales calls and you live in a CRM, this is the pick. Reported 2026 pricing is genuinely confusing across sources — figures range from ~$10 to ~$18/month for paid tiers, with a usable free tier. Confirm current pricing before budgeting. The trade-off for the automation depth: more setup than Otter or Fathom.

3. Fathom — best free tier

Fathom's free tier is the story: unlimited recording and transcription, with a handful of AI summaries per month — enough that many small teams never pay. Paid tiers (~$19–$24/month in 2026 reports) add unlimited AI summaries, CRM sync, and sales-oriented features like deal views and coaching insights. 4. tl;dv — best for clips and compliance

tl;dv (too long; didn't view) differentiates on two axes: timestamped shareable clips that make meeting moments portable, and enterprise-grade compliance controls (SSO, retention policies, EU data residency options). It's the pick for larger or regulated teams that need recording infrastructure, not just notes. Reported 2026 pricing: free tier plus paid at roughly $18–$29 per seat/month depending on source and billing. 5. Granola — best if you hate meeting bots

Granola's pitch is simple: no bot joins your call. It captures audio from your device, merges it with the notes you typed during the meeting, and produces polished summaries — the result feels like your own notes, enhanced. For client-facing calls where a "Granola AI has joined" announcement feels unprofessional, this is the answer. Reported 2026 pricing: free for 25 meetings, paid around $12–$14/month. The documented trade-off in 2026 comparisons: there's no replayable saved recording in the way bot-based tools offer — it's a notes tool, not a recording archive.

6. Tactiq — best lightweight option

Tactiq is a Chrome extension rather than a platform: it captures live captions from Google Meet, Zoom, and Teams and turns them into summaries, action items, and AI answers about the meeting. No bot, minimal setup, and the cheapest paid tier on this list (reported ~$8–$12/month, with a usable free tier). It's the right pick if you want meeting AI without adopting another dashboard. The trade-off is depth: it won't replace Fireflies' CRM automation or tl;dv's compliance controls.

Privacy and consent

This matters more here than in any other category on this site. Key points:

  • Consent laws vary. Some jurisdictions require all parties' consent to record; others require one. Know your local law and your attendees' locations.
  • Disclose regardless. Even where one-party consent is legal, tell attendees the meeting is being recorded and transcribed. Every tool here shows an indicator — don't try to hide it.
  • Check company policy. Some employers and clients prohibit third-party recording tools. When in doubt, ask.
  • Data retention. Transcripts contain sensitive business discussion. Review each vendor's retention, deletion, and data-residency options — tl;dv and enterprise tiers of others offer the most control.
  • Client calls deserve extra care. This is exactly Granola's use case: when a visible bot feels wrong, a bot-free tool plus explicit verbal disclosure is the professional combination.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best free AI meeting assistant?

Fathom — its free tier includes unlimited recording and transcription, which is more generous than any competitor's free plan. Otter.ai's free tier (300 minutes per month) is the best second choice for light users. Both work without a credit card, so the cheapest way to test is to run Fathom on your next two meetings.

Do I need to tell people the meeting is being recorded?

Yes — always disclose it. Many jurisdictions legally require all-party consent, and company policies often require disclosure regardless. Every tool on this list shows a recording indicator, but don't rely on the icon alone: announce it out loud at the start of the meeting so there's no ambiguity about consent.

Otter.ai vs Fireflies.ai — which is better?

Otter is the better general-purpose assistant (transcription quality, mobile, in-person). Fireflies is better for sales teams that need CRM automation and deal intelligence. For a typical remote team without a CRM-centric workflow, Otter (or Fathom free) is the simpler choice.

Can I use a meeting assistant without a bot joining the call?

Yes — Granola records from your device with no bot, and Tactiq works as a browser extension capturing live captions. Both avoid the "AI has joined the meeting" announcement that some find unprofessional on client calls. If client perception matters, bot-free recording keeps the meeting feeling human while you still get the notes.

Are AI meeting notes accurate enough to rely on?

Transcription from the leading tools is highly accurate for clear audio in supported languages. AI summaries are good at capturing decisions and action items but can miss nuance or misattribute statements — skim the summary against the transcript for anything contractual or contentious.

For most remote teams, the answer is simple: start with Fathom's free tier. Unlimited recording and transcription at $0 removes the entire cost question, and you'll learn what you actually need.
