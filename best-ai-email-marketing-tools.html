Every email platform now claims to be "AI-powered." Strip away the marketing and the AI inside these tools does a handful of concrete things: generating subject lines and email drafts, optimizing send times, predicting which subscribers are most likely to engage, and segmenting audiences automatically. Useful — but not magic, and not a reason to choose a platform by itself.

So we evaluated seven leading email tools the way we always do: real pricing at realistic list sizes, documented features, automation depth, and aggregated user sentiment. The AI gets a fair hearing in each review — as a feature, not a headline.

The 7 tools, reviewed

1. GetResponse — Best overall

GetResponse earns our top pick the unglamorous way: more automation per dollar than anything else here, pricing that scales gently as your list grows, and bundled extras (webinars, funnels, landing pages) that competitors charge separately for. Its AI email generator handles first drafts and subject lines competently.

Strengths: deep visual automation from the $59/mo tier; 1,000-contact billing blocks scale better than Mailchimp's 500s; webinar hosting bundled in upper plans; solid free plan to start.

Watch out: the interface is denser than Mailchimp's — beginners may need a week to feel at home; template design isn't its strongest suit.

Try GetResponse

2. Kit (formerly ConvertKit) — Best for creators

Kit is built for one audience — creators with newsletters, courses, and digital products — and it shows in every design decision: simple sequences, visual automations that don't require a marketing degree, and commerce features for selling directly to subscribers. Its free plan (up to 10,000 subscribers, per their current offering) is among the most generous in the industry.

Strengths: creator-first workflows; generous free tier; clean, fast interface; good deliverability reputation among creators.

Watch out: email design templates are deliberately plain (creators mostly send text emails — fine for newsletters, less so for designed campaigns); paid pricing has been restructured, so verify current tiers.

Try Kit free

3. MailerLite — Best free plan

MailerLite's free plan (up to 1,000 subscribers) is the real deal — not a crippled demo but a usable email tool with automation included. Paid plans start around $10/mo, making it one of the cheapest ways to run a serious newsletter. Its AI writing assistant and smart-sending features cover the basics well.

Strengths: best genuinely-usable free tier; clean modern UI; straightforward pricing; good for newsletters and simple automation.

Watch out: advanced automation and ecommerce depth lag the top picks; account approval can be strict (a deliverability choice, but expect verification steps).

Try MailerLite free

4. ActiveCampaign — Best automation

If email automation is the entire reason you're buying a tool, ActiveCampaign is the answer. Its automation builder is the deepest here — conditional logic, lead scoring, CRM baked in, predictive sending and predictive content that genuinely adapt to subscriber behavior. This is the power user's platform, and it's priced like one starting around $15/mo for the Starter tier.

Strengths: best-in-class automation and segmentation; built-in CRM; predictive AI features are actually useful; scales to serious marketing operations.

Watch out: steeper learning curve than everything else on this list; no free plan (trial only); costs climb with contacts and add-ons — verify current tier pricing carefully.

Try ActiveCampaign

5. Mailchimp — Easiest to start

Mailchimp remains the easiest email tool to start with, full stop: the onboarding, the templates, the plain-language UI. Intuit Assist adds AI subject lines, send-time optimization, and creative assistance. The trade-off, as ever, is the pricing curve — 500-contact billing blocks get expensive as your list grows, so it's best for lists that stay small.

Strengths: smoothest onboarding in the category; huge template and integration library; Intuit ecosystem (QuickBooks) synergy.

Watch out: pricing scales aggressively with list size; advanced automation costs more here than at GetResponse or ActiveCampaign.

Try Mailchimp

6. Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) — Best budget all-rounder

Brevo's unusual pricing is its superpower: plans are based on email volume, not contact count, so a large list with infrequent sends stays cheap. The free tier (300 emails/day) suits low-volume senders, and paid plans start at a genuinely low entry point. It also bundles SMS and chat, which few competitors do at this price.

Strengths: volume-based pricing favors big-list/light-senders; SMS built in; solid automation for the price; European data-center options.

Watch out: pricing has been restructured — verify current tiers; template design and UX trail Mailchimp; daily send limits on lower tiers need watching.

Try Brevo

7. Constant Contact — Best for events & nonprofits

Constant Contact is the veteran pick for organizations that live on events: nonprofits, clubs, local businesses. Event management, registrations, and surveys are baked in rather than bolted on, and the interface is deliberately simple. AI content suggestions cover the basics. Pricing starts around $12/mo for the Lite plan.

Strengths: event tools and surveys included; very simple to learn; good nonprofit discounts (verify current program); reliable support reputation.

Watch out: no free plan (trial only); automation depth lags the top picks; design feels dated next to Mailchimp.

Try Constant Contact

How should you choose the right email marketing tool?

Pick by three things: list size (under 2,000 contacts nearly any tool works; over 5,000 the billing-block math decides for you), automation needs (complex journeys → ActiveCampaign or GetResponse; weekly newsletter → Kit or MailerLite), and starting point (every tool here except ActiveCampaign and Constant Contact has a free tier or trial). Here they are:

  1. How big will your list be in 12 months? Under 2,000 contacts, nearly any of these work and price differences are small. Over 5,000, the billing-block math (GetResponse's 1,000s vs. Mailchimp's 500s, Brevo's volume pricing) starts deciding for you.
  2. Is automation the job, or is sending the job? If you need complex journeys, scoring, and CRM — ActiveCampaign or GetResponse. If you need a newsletter that goes out weekly — Kit or MailerLite.
  3. What's your actual starting point? Every tool here except ActiveCampaign and Constant Contact has a free tier or trial. Start free, send real campaigns for a month, and let your own usage tell you which constraints chafe first. That's worth more than any review — including this one.

Frequently asked questions

What does "AI" actually do in these email tools?

Five concrete things: generating subject lines, drafting email copy, optimizing send times per subscriber, predicting engagement likelihood, and auto-segmenting audiences. All seven tools do at least two of these. None of them replaces strategy, list building, or good offers — AI makes a working email program faster, not a broken one functional.

Which tool is cheapest for a large list?

Usually Brevo (volume-based pricing) or GetResponse (gentler per-contact scaling) — but "usually" isn't a quote. Run your actual list size and monthly send volume through each pricing calculator; the answer flips depending on whether you're contact-heavy or send-heavy. Your own numbers, not a generic ranking, decide this one every time.

Can I switch tools later without losing my list?

Yes — exporting/importing contacts via CSV is standard everywhere, so your list itself is safe. What doesn't transfer: automations, templates, and historical analytics. Switching costs are real but manageable; they're lowest when you switch early, which is another argument for starting on a free plan before you invest in complex sequences.

Do I need AI features at all?

Honestly? Not to start. The unglamorous fundamentals — a clean opt-in list, a consistent sending schedule, one good welcome sequence — outperform AI subject lines every time. Add AI as an accelerator once the basics work. AI multiplies a working program; it doesn't fix a broken one, so buy the program first and the AI second.

Why isn't there a single "best" pick?

Because a solo creator sending a weekly newsletter and a 20-person ecommerce team running abandoned-cart flows have almost nothing in common as buyers. Any review that crowns one winner for both is simplifying past the point of usefulness. Our per-use-case picks above are the honest version.

Final verdict

If you want one default answer: start with GetResponse's free plan (or MailerLite's, if your needs are newsletter-simple). Both give you room to grow into automation without the pricing shocks, and both let you validate that email earns its keep before you spend anything.
