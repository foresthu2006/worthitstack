How Rips by Triumph works

Rips by Triumph is an app (iOS and Android, US, 18+) devoted entirely to collectible cards. Packs run from $1 to $1,000, with the odds and the minimum and maximum pull values scaled to the price; new users get a free Pokémon starter pack after funding an account with $10 or more, and referrals earn more free packs. Pull a card and you can keep it in your collection, have it shipped, or take Triumph's buyback offer. Two things define the experience. First, verification: Triumph requires KYC — a government-issued ID, a selfie, and possibly more documents, sometimes with a manual review of about 24 hours — and you cannot ship cards or withdraw funds until you pass it. Second, the asset itself: a card's value is an estimate set by a volatile secondary market, and it moves with grading, condition, and hype cycles that have nothing to do with the pack you bought.

How re:COVET works

re:COVET (recovet.co, by Curated Drops, LLC) is the reveal format applied to luxury goods. You choose a designer, then a box. Each box draws from pre-owned, vintage and new pieces from that designer, in five tiers, and the price, the minimum fair market value, the full assortment and the item selection probability of each tier are published before purchase; pieces are valued at the supplier's price or the last sold price, and many are vintage or one-of-one. Boxes are bought from a prepaid balance — money you add can't be withdrawn, only money from a sell-back can — and the piece is revealed in the app. You can ship it, authenticated by a third party before it is packed, by courier to US addresses outside Washington and Connecticut ($15 per shipment, plus 1% insurance on pieces of $500 or more), or accept a buyback offer if the house makes one.

Where Rips by Triumph genuinely wins

For card people, Triumph is thoughtfully built. The pack range lets you rip for a dollar or a thousand, US payment rails (including Venmo and ACH) are friendlier than the crypto-first norm in this industry, and the starter and referral packs make the first rip cheap. If you collect Pokémon or NBA cards and enjoy the rip itself, it is a legitimate, enjoyable way to do it from your phone.

Where re:COVET pulls ahead

A trading card is a price with a picture on it. Its worth lives in a market feed — population reports, grading queues, the next set release. A designer piece is different in kind: authenticated by a third party before it is packed, valued by a stated method — the supplier's price or the last sold price — and useful whether or not a market ever looks at it again. re:COVET makes no promise about what a piece would fetch later: fair market value is the house's estimate, and buyback offers are at the house's discretion. If the reveal is the fun but the object is the point, re:COVET is the one that ends with a piece from a designer you chose.
