A good chatbot answers the questions your visitors ask at 11pm, captures the lead, and books the call — without you hiring anyone. A bad one frustrates three people and teaches everyone else to email you instead. The difference is rarely the AI model; it's whether the bot is trained on your business and whether there's a clean handoff to a human.

Small businesses generally need one of three things: a website bot that deflects support questions (Tidio, Chatbase), a social DM automation machine (ManyChat), or a support platform that scales with a team (Intercom, Crisp, Freshchat). Match the tool to the job first, the price second.

Deep dive: each chatbot

1. Tidio — best for most small businesses

Tidio bundles live chat, helpdesk ticketing, and Lyro — its AI agent that answers from your FAQs and knowledge base — in the simplest setup in the category. The free tier (50 conversations/month) is genuinely enough for a local business getting a few dozen chats monthly, and paid plans start around $24–$29/month. Reviewers consistently call it the easiest chatbot a non-technical owner can deploy in an afternoon. Limitations: it's a small-business tool — conversation limits scale costs, and enterprises will outgrow it. If you've never had a chatbot, start here.

Visit Tidio

2. Chatbase — best budget custom Q&A bot

Chatbase's pitch is refreshingly narrow: upload your website, docs, or FAQ, and get a GPT-powered chatbot that answers from your content. No bloated platform, no seat math — just a bot that knows your business. Reported 2026 pricing starts around $19–$32/month depending on source and billing, with a limited free tier. It's the right pick when your need is specifically "answer questions about our products/services accurately." The trade-off: it's a Q&A bot, not a full support platform — no native live-chat team workflow or CRM depth.

Visit Chatbase

3. ManyChat — best for Instagram and Facebook DMs

ManyChat doesn't compete on website chat — it owns social DM automation. Comment-to-DM triggers ("comment GUIDE and I'll send it"), story reply automation, lead capture flows, and broadcast messaging across Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and TikTok. For businesses whose leads come through social, it's close to essential infrastructure. Reported 2026 pricing: free up to 1,000 contacts, Pro at $39/month ($29 annual) adding AI features and WhatsApp, with an active-contact pricing model that can create overage costs as you grow. The caveat: you're building on rented land — every automation must follow each platform's messaging policies, which change.

Visit ManyChat

4. Intercom — best when accuracy justifies the cost

Intercom's Fin AI agent is the resolution-quality leader — 2026 comparisons credit it with the highest automated resolution rates in the category. The pricing model is the story: roughly $0.99 per resolved conversation on top of per-seat plans (reported ~$29–$74/seat/month depending on tier). For a small business, that math only works at real support volume — but when it works, it's the closest thing to hiring a support rep that never sleeps. Overkill for most small sites; the right call when support accuracy directly affects revenue.

Visit Intercom

5. Crisp — best all-in-one value

Crisp bundles chatbot, live chat, shared inbox, built-in CRM, and knowledge base at ~$25/month per workspace (with a free tier). If you're currently stitching together separate tools for chat and customer management, Crisp consolidates them. Its MagicReply AI drafts responses for agents rather than fully automating — a sensible middle ground. Reviewers rate it the best value for growing teams that want one workspace. Less specialized than Tidio for pure simplicity, less powerful than Intercom for pure AI — the balanced pick.

Visit Crisp

6. HubSpot chatbot — best if you already use HubSpot

If your CRM is HubSpot, use HubSpot's native chatbot before adding any vendor. It's free with HubSpot CRM, conversations land directly in your contact records, and handoffs to sales are native. The AI capabilities are more basic than dedicated bot platforms — rule-based flows with AI assistance rather than a true AI agent — but the integration value outweighs that for HubSpot shops. Don't pay for Tidio or Intercom to sit awkwardly on top of HubSpot unless you've outgrown the native bot.

Visit HubSpot

7. Freshchat — best affordable omnichannel support

Freshchat (by Freshworks) brings website, mobile, WhatsApp, and Facebook Messenger into one dashboard with Freddy AI handling routing, response suggestions, and auto-resolution. Reported pricing from ~$19/agent/month with a free tier. It's the pick for support-heavy small businesses — 100+ tickets a week — that need omnichannel coverage without Intercom's per-resolution pricing. Lighter businesses will find Tidio simpler; heavier ones may still graduate to Intercom.

Visit Freshchat

Setup advice that actually matters

  • Train it on your real questions. Pull your 20 most-asked questions from email and DMs. A bot trained on your actual FAQ beats a clever bot trained on nothing.
  • Always offer a human escape hatch. "Talk to a human" should be one tap away, always. Bots that trap visitors create the negative reviews.
  • Start narrower than you think. Launch answering 10 questions perfectly rather than 100 questions badly. Expand from real conversation logs.
  • Review transcripts weekly at first. The first month's chat logs are a goldmine — they show what visitors actually ask and where the bot fails.
  • Under ~20 conversations a week? You may not need a chatbot yet. A good FAQ page and fast email replies beat a half-configured bot.

Frequently asked questions

What's the best free chatbot for a small business website?

Tidio — 50 conversations/month free with the Lyro AI agent included is enough for most small sites. Chatbase and Crisp also offer limited free tiers; ManyChat is free up to 1,000 contacts for social DMs. Start on Tidio's free tier to test whether chat converts your visitors; only pay once the conversation volume justifies a plan.

How much does a small business chatbot cost?

Most small businesses land between $0 and $50/month: Tidio (~$24–$29), Chatbase (~$19–$32), Crisp (~$25), or ManyChat ($39 Pro). Costs scale with conversation volume. Intercom is the outlier — per-resolution pricing makes sense only at real support volume. Budget for the tier your volume actually needs, not the cheapest headline price.

Will a chatbot hurt my customer experience?

A bad one will — bots that can't answer and won't hand off to a human are worse than no bot. The fix is structural: train on your real FAQs, always offer a human handoff, and review transcripts weekly for the first month.

ManyChat vs Tidio — which should I get?

Different jobs. ManyChat automates Instagram, Facebook, and WhatsApp DMs; Tidio handles website chat. If your leads come through social channels, get ManyChat. If they come through your website, get Tidio. Many businesses eventually run both — the tools complement rather than replace each other.

Do I need a chatbot if I already use HubSpot?

Probably not a separate one — HubSpot's native chatbot is free with the CRM and logs every conversation to contact records, which covers basic lead capture and FAQ automation. Add a dedicated platform only when you've outgrown the native bot's AI capabilities and need multi-channel automation the native bot can't handle.

Final verdict

For most small businesses, Tidio is the right starting point: free to try, simple to set up, and capable enough to handle real FAQ volume. Add ManyChat if social DMs drive your leads, graduate to Crisp or Freshchat when support becomes a team sport, and consider Intercom only when resolution accuracy is worth paying per conversation.

The unifying rule: the bot is only as good as its training. Whichever you choose, feed it your real questions, keep the human handoff one tap away, and read the transcripts. Do that, and even the cheapest option on this list will outperform an expensive bot left on autopilot.
