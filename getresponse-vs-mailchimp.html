GetResponse and Mailchimp are the two names that come up in almost every "which email tool?" conversation — and they represent two genuinely different philosophies. Mailchimp is the friendly giant: famously easy to start, beautifully designed, now part of Intuit. GetResponse is the veteran workhorse: around since the late 1990s, deeper automation at lower prices, and a habit of bundling extras (like webinars) that competitors charge separately for.

Most comparisons declare a single winner and move on. We won't do that, because the honest answer depends on your list size, your budget, and how much automation you actually need. Here's the full breakdown.

The questions people ask most about this comparison, answered directly. Stats and pricing verified October 2026.

GetResponse vs Mailchimp — which is cheaper as my list grows?

GetResponse gets relatively cheaper: its Email Marketing plan starts from $19/month for 1,000 contacts with gentler per-contact pricing as you grow. Mailchimp Essentials starts from $13/month for 500 contacts but its contact-based scaling is steeper. Beginners save on Mailchimp; growing lists save on GetResponse.

Which has better automation: GetResponse or Mailchimp?

GetResponse — deeper automation per dollar, with webinars bundled into the same tool. Mailchimp wins on templates, ease of use, and the Intuit ecosystem, but automation depth is GetResponse's territory.

Is GetResponse or Mailchimp better for a complete beginner?

Mailchimp — the easiest start, prettiest templates, and the Intuit ecosystem make it the friendliest onboarding. The caveat: if you're a beginner who plans to grow a list, GetResponse's $0-for-500-contacts free plan plus its webinar bundle may be the cheaper long-term home.

Does GetResponse really include webinars?

Pick GetResponse if your list is going to grow, you want serious automation without paying premium-tier prices, or you'd like webinars bundled into the same tool. Its pricing scales more gently as your contacts increase.

Pick Mailchimp if you want the easiest possible start, care about design polish and templates, or live inside the Intuit ecosystem (QuickBooks, etc.). It's the smoother on-ramp — you just pay more as you scale.

The one-sentence version: GetResponse wins on value and automation depth; Mailchimp wins on simplicity and ecosystem. Growing lists should lean GetResponse.

Pricing is where this comparison gets interesting — and where most reviews mislead you by quoting only the cheapest tier. Both vendors charge by contact count and raise prices as your list grows, but they count very differently: Mailchimp bills in 500-contact blocks, GetResponse in 1,000-contact blocks. That structural difference matters more than the headline prices.

Ease of use

Mailchimp's edge — and it's a real one. Mailchimp practically invented friendly email software onboarding: the signup flow, the template picker, the plain-language guidance. If you've never sent a marketing email, you'll be sending one faster in Mailchimp. This is our assessment based on consistently reported user sentiment, not a lab test — but the pattern across reviews is unmistakable.

GetResponse isn't hard, exactly — it's just denser. More menus, more options, more power-user surface area. The trade-off is deliberate: that density is where the deeper automation lives. Beginners sometimes bounce off it; marketers who outgrow Mailchimp often land here.

Automation depth

GetResponse's edge — the biggest gap in this comparison. GetResponse's visual workflow builder (available from the $59 Marketing Automation tier) supports genuinely complex branching: tag-based triggers, scoring, multi-path customer journeys. It's the kind of automation that usually costs much more elsewhere.

Mailchimp's Customer Journey Builder (Standard, from $20) is good for straightforward sequences — welcome series, abandoned carts, post-purchase follow-ups. But the more advanced branching and segmentation depth costs more, and power users consistently report hitting ceilings sooner. If automation is the reason you're buying an email tool, GetResponse gives you more of it per dollar.

AI features

Both vendors now ship AI features, and both are worth a clear-eyed look rather than excitement:

  • GetResponse: AI email generator (drafts full emails from a prompt), AI subject-line suggestions. Useful for beating blank-page syndrome.
  • Mailchimp (Intuit Assist): AI-assisted subject lines and send-time optimization, plus creative assistant features that auto-generate branded designs.

The honest take: these are genuinely useful accelerators, not reasons to choose a platform. Nobody should pick an email tool because of its AI subject-line writer — pick it for automation depth, pricing at your scale, and deliverability, and treat the AI as a bonus. (Feature names and availability change often; verify what's current.)

Webinars, landing pages & extras

Webinars are GetResponse's quiet superpower. Webinar hosting is bundled into its upper-tier plans — if you run webinars, this alone can replace a separate $50–100/mo webinar tool. No other major email platform in this price range does this. Mailchimp has nothing comparable; you'd integrate a third-party tool.

Both include landing page builders and signup forms that are perfectly serviceable for lead capture. Neither will replace a dedicated landing-page tool, but both mean you don't strictly need one to start.

Deliverability & analytics

Both platforms operate dedicated deliverability infrastructure and offer the standard analytics suite: opens, clicks, bounces, unsubscribes, and A/B testing. Here's what we won't do: quote inbox-placement percentages as if they're stable facts. Independent deliverability tests vary by sender, list hygiene, and sending volume — your list hygiene matters more than which of these two you pick. Either platform, used well with a clean opt-in list, will land in inboxes fine.

Quick answers

Is GetResponse cheaper than Mailchimp for growing lists?

Generally yes. GetResponse's Email Marketing tier starts around $19/month for 1,000 contacts, versus Mailchimp Essentials around $13/month for 500 — but Mailchimp's per-contact pricing climbs steeply as your list grows, while GetResponse scales more gently. Under ~2,000 contacts the gap is small; beyond that, GetResponse usually wins. Confirm current pricing before buying.

Which is easier for a beginner — GetResponse or Mailchimp?

Mailchimp. Its onboarding is unmatched, its templates are the prettiest in the category, and the Intuit ecosystem is a real plus if you use QuickBooks. GetResponse trades some hand-holding for deeper automation per dollar. Beginners should lean Mailchimp; senders whose lists are about to grow should lean GetResponse.

Does GetResponse include webinars?

Yes. Webinar hosting is bundled into GetResponse plans — unusual among email tools and a genuine part of its value case against Mailchimp, where you'd pay for a separate webinar platform. If you run webinars to grow your list, that single inclusion can swing the comparison on its own.

Frequently asked questions

Is GetResponse really cheaper than Mailchimp?

At very small list sizes, no — Mailchimp's $13/mo Essentials undercuts GetResponse's $19/mo entry tier. The crossover happens as your list grows: Mailchimp bills in 500-contact blocks while GetResponse bills in 1,000-contact blocks, so GetResponse's per-contact cost scales more gently. Run your projected 12-month list size through both calculators.

Does Mailchimp still have a free plan?

Yes — Mailchimp's free plan covers up to 500 contacts and 1,000 sends per month, with Mailchimp branding on emails and limited features including limited support. It's genuinely usable for validating an idea before you pay anything; the paid tiers remove branding and unlock automation. Validate free, then upgrade only when sends or automation justify it.

Which is better for a complete beginner?

Mailchimp. Its onboarding flow, template library, and plain-language UI remain the easiest on-ramp in email marketing. GetResponse isn't difficult, but it assumes slightly more marketing familiarity. If you've never sent a campaign before, start with Mailchimp and only switch when you outgrow its automation or its pricing.

Which has better marketing automation?

GetResponse, by a clear margin at comparable prices. Its visual workflow builder (from the $59/month tier) supports deeper branching, contact scoring, and multi-path journeys than Mailchimp offers below its premium tiers. If automation is your main buying criterion, this category alone justifies the shortlist.

Can I migrate my list from one to the other?

Yes — both support CSV list imports, and both publish migration guides for switching platforms. Expect to rebuild automations and templates manually; there's no one-click migration of workflows between the two. Budget an afternoon for a small list with simple sequences, and longer when complex automations need rebuilding.

Are the AI features worth paying for?

Honestly? They're nice accelerators, not decision-drivers. AI subject lines and send-time optimization save time, but the core value of either platform is still automation depth, pricing at your scale, and your own list hygiene. Don't pick a platform for its AI writer.

Final verdict

For most small businesses with growth ambitions, GetResponse is the better buy — deeper automation per dollar, gentler pricing as your list scales, and bundled webinars that replace a separate subscription. For beginners who want the smoothest start, the prettiest templates, and the Intuit ecosystem, Mailchimp remains excellent — just go in eyes open about where its pricing curve goes.

Either way: start on a free plan, validate that email actually drives revenue for you, and only then commit to a paid tier at your real list size.
