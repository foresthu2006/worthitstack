The credit math that changes everything

This is the section that decides whether HeyGen is worth it for you, because one finished minute of video costs very different amounts depending on which avatar model you render:

Avatar III (the standard model): ~7 credits per finished minute. On the Creator plan's 600 credits, that's roughly 85 minutes of video a month. Generous — more than most small teams produce.

Avatar IV (the premium look most people actually want): ~16–20 credits per finished minute. Those same 600 credits now buy about 30 minutes a month. Still fine for a weekly marketer — but a different product than the Avatar III math suggests.

Avatar V (the newest, most photorealistic): ~48 credits per finished minute. Creator's 600 credits buy about 12 minutes. The model choice alone changes your effective cost per finished minute by a factor of seven.

Two more rules that affect the bill: failed or re-rendered clips still consume credits, so a bad generation isn't free; and unused credits roll over for only one billing cycle on monthly plans (they accumulate across the year on annual). Credits don't transfer or get refunded when you drop back to free.

The honest read: the Creator plan is plenty of runway at Avatar III quality and still reasonable at Avatar IV — but if everything you ship will be Avatar V, you're a Pro-plan buyer, not a Creator-plan buyer. Budget by finished premium minutes per month, not by the $29 headline, and there are no surprises.

The free plan's real limits

The free tier is genuinely useful as a test drive: 3 videos a month (up to 1 minute each) at 720p with a watermark, enough to judge whether the avatars and voices suit your brand. Reviewers consistently say onboarding-to-first-video takes minutes.

The limits are calibrated to convert: the watermark (gone on Creator+), 720p cap (1080p on paid), and no voice cloning or custom avatars. For anyone evaluating the tech rather than shipping content, the free plan answers the only question that matters at that stage — do the avatars look and sound good enough for my brand?

What it actually does

Avatar library. Hundreds of stock avatars — more looks, settings, and wardrobe variety than any competitor, which matters when you need a presenter who suits a specific brand. This is consistently cited as the category's deepest library.

Custom digital twins. Record yourself once (webcam or phone footage), and generate new videos of yourself delivering new scripts without filming again. Instant avatars for everyday use; higher-fidelity custom avatars on upper tiers.

Translation and dubbing across 175+ languages. Not just audio-swapped — the lip sync is re-timed to the translated audio, plus a multilingual embeddable player. This is the feature teams use to localize marketing campaigns and training content without re-filming.

Marketing workflow. Templates, brand kit, prompt-to-video, interactive avatars for embeds, 4K rendering (Pro+), and a full API for programmatic video pipelines (Team plan and up). On Business: SCORM export for learning platforms and connectors for Zapier, Make, n8n, and HubSpot.

What it doesn't do well: native voiceover. Reviewers consistently describe HeyGen's voices as the weak link next to the avatars — most serious users pair it with ElevenLabs or a voice-cloning service. The product is an avatar engine, not a voice engine.

The three catches

Catch 1: the headline price and the real price differ by up to 7x. Covered above — the credit math. Most of the billing backlash online comes from users who bought Creator for the premium Avatar IV/V look and discovered their credits evaporate in weeks. This is avoidable once you know the rule, and unavoidable if you don't.

Catch 2: downgrading requires emailing support. There is no self-service downgrade click in billing settings, per consistent reviewer reports. Upgrading is one click; downgrading is an email. Plan your first month as a real trial and set a calendar reminder — or just start monthly, not annual.

Catch 3: weak native voices. The avatars are best-in-class; the voices are mid-tier. If your content is voice-forward (narration, ads), budget for a voice tool or voice-cloning add-on on top. If your content is presenter-forward (talking-head explainers, multilingual training), this catch barely matters.

Who should buy HeyGen?

Buy it if: you need a person on screen this week without a camera — marketing videos, product explainers, training libraries, multilingual localization, or faceless content channels. The avatar library and digital twins are genuinely best-in-class, and the free plan lets you verify the look before paying.

Don't buy it if: you only want the premium Avatar IV/V aesthetic on a $29 budget (see the credit math — you're a $49/month Pro buyer minimum); you need strong native voiceover out of the box; or per-credit billing stresses you out — Synthesia's minute-based pricing is calmer and starts cheaper ($29/month, annual billing available).

The localization play is the product's hidden superpower. If you already have footage or scripts and need them in 10 languages, HeyGen's translation-with-retimed-lip-sync is priced against re-filming ($29–$49/month vs. a video shoot) — the easiest value argument on the whole pricing page.
