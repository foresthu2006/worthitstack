Every Luxury Mystery Box Site, Ranked for 2027

Last updated November 2026 · 10 min read · Facts checked November 2026

Disclosure: re:COVET, a luxury mystery box that has not opened yet, is a sister project from the same team behind Worth It Stack. We scored it by the same method as every other site, from its own Terms and site, and it ties for first. Because it hasn't opened, its score reflects what it publishes, not what we could check in operation. None of the other sites pays us.

In short: We checked 14 live luxury mystery box sites that sell to US buyers against their own pages and ranked 13 of them, plus our sister project re:COVET, on how much they tell you before and after you pay. re:COVET, which hasn't opened yet, and StockX Mystery Boxes tie for first on 10 of 12, followed by Metadraw and MrLoot. One thing barely changes with rank: on the seven sites where we worked it out from published odds, an opening returns less than the box costs in listed value, about 83–91% of the price on most of them.

The field is smaller and more alike than it looks. Eight of the 14 sites are run by Cyprus companies, three of them from the same Limassol address. None states a gaming licence, and none names an independent authenticator. Most sell boxes named after a house, such as Rolex, Hermès or Louis Vuitton, where most openings are something else. What separates them is what they are willing to put in writing.

The quick verdict

Top of the ranking: re:COVET (our sister project, not yet open) and StockX Mystery Boxes, tied on 10/12. re:COVET publishes tier probabilities, a floor and its valuation method but no sell-back rate; StockX publishes floor, average and ceiling and verifies items itself, but has only one designer-led box and pays sell-backs in StockX Credit.

Most open live luxury-first site: Metadraw, 9/12. Odds and a value range on every box, and its excluded states listed.

Plainest exit: PackDraw (100% of an item's value, as balance or crypto) and Royale (item value less 0.5% in crypto).

The one-sentence version: Rank tells you how much a site discloses, not whether a box is a good buy, and on the seven sites where we did the math, it isn't one on average.

How we ranked them

Each site was scored on six questions, 0, 1 or 2 each, using only its own pages: its site, help centre, terms, app-store listing or official blog, read on 2 November 2026. Where a live site blocked automated reads, we used archive.org snapshots of its own pages and say so. If a site's pages don't say it, it scores 0. re:COVET, which hasn't opened, was scored the same way from its Terms and site. Ties share a rank and are listed by score, then by name.

  1. Odds. Are odds shown per item or per tier before you pay? 2 if the site says where; 1 if it only claims odds exist.
  2. Range. Does each box state a floor, a value range or the house edge? 2 if stated; 1 if it can be read off published item values.
  3. Valuation. Does the site say how item values are set? 2 for a method with a named data source; 1 for a described basis.
  4. Auth. (authentication). 2 for the site's own documented verification or a named authenticator; 1 for a claim with no authenticator named; 0 for no claim, or a claim its own terms disclaim.
  5. Exit. 1 if a sell-back rate is stated anywhere; 1 more if sell-back money can leave the site as cash, bank transfer or crypto.
  6. Operator. 1 for a named operator with its jurisdiction; 1 more for a list of the US states it excludes.

This ranks disclosure, not value. How much a box returns is in its own section, because on that measure the sites are much closer together than their marketing suggests.

The ranking, site by site

=1. re:COVET (our sister project, not yet open) — 10/12

re:COVET (Curated Drops, LLC, Delaware) is our sister project and has not opened yet, so it is scored from its Terms and site rather than from boxes we could see in operation. Its Terms say every box lists its price, minimum fair market value and full assortment in five tiers, with the item selection probability of each tier, before you buy, and that fair market value is the supplier's price for the piece or, where no supplier lists it, the last sold price. Its site says a piece is authenticated by a third party, not named, before it is packed. It is not available in Washington or Connecticut.

Where it scores lower: the house may offer to buy a piece back at an amount it sets, with no published rate, and money you add can't be refunded or withdrawn; only sell-back money can go to a US bank, after Stripe's identity checks. Like most of the boxes here, it prices a box above the average fair market value of what is inside. Its list is at recovet.co.

=1. StockX Mystery Boxes — 10/12

StockX, the Detroit resale marketplace, launched Mystery Boxes in the US on 9 July 2026. Only one of its 16 boxes ($10 to $400) is designer-led: the $300 Accessories box, with a published floor of $300, an average of $361 and a ceiling of $3,000. Its 74% tier shows pieces like a Dior card holder or Cartier sunglasses; a Chanel Reissue 2.55 or Lady Dior sits in the 0.5% tier.

It is the only site here that says how it values items (its own sales data plus a pricing model) and verifies them itself. The catch is the exit: sell-back pays StockX Credit, not cash, at a rate its own pages give three ways (75–90%, 80–90%, up to 90%). Miss the roughly five-minute decision window and it charges your card and ships the item. The iOS app that sells the boxes is rated 13+, and the box pages state no minimum age.

3. Metadraw — 9/12

Metadraw (METADRAW LTD, Nicosia, Cyprus) runs a site and iOS and Android apps with a Luxury category of 16 boxes from $19 to $9,999, including Rolex, Louis Vuitton, Dior, Goyard and Gucci boxes. Every box shows per-item rates and a minimum-to-maximum value range, and its terms list nine excluded states: AZ, CO, MD, NE, NM, ND, VT, NJ and TN.

Values are a "dynamic market price", and its authentication "third-party partners" are not named. Its terms say there is no cash-out, while the site offers crypto withdrawal of an item's "recycle" value; the same terms repeatedly describe a "Center Royale Shopping Game", which reads like another operator's boilerplate. Montres Rolex ($1,299) gives a Rolex 5.64% of the time; every other opening is a $199 Casio or a $367.40 MoonSwatch.

4. MrLoot — 8/12

MrLoot (Millerdale & Kingston Ventures LTD, Larnaca, Cyprus) has a Luxury section running from a $1 "1 Dollar Rolex" to an $11,657 Rolex box, with designer bag, Cartier jewelry and designer fashion boxes in between. Box pages show per-item odds and a "minimum guaranteed value", though that minimum can be met in non-cash XP. It says item values are its own fulfilment "ceiling", "not necessarily the market price", and it bars nine states: CA, CT, ID, MI, MT, NV, NJ, NY and WA.

The exit is its weakest point: sell-back pays site credit at no stated rate, and crypto settlement is at MrLoot's discretion after ID checks. The box data its pages load, though not shown on screen, carries a margin field: 0.10 on the Rolex box and about 0.20 on Big Bags.

=5. Cases.gg — 7/12

Cases.gg (CGG Entertainment Ltd, Nicosia, with payments through CasesGG US LLC in Delaware) runs on a sweepstakes coin model, with Rolex, Chanel, Dior, Cartier, Gucci and Louis Vuitton boxes from about $224 to $10,056. It is the only site in this ranking that states its house edge, 10% on cases, and every item carries a published ticket range. It excludes Idaho, Montana, Michigan, Nevada and Washington.

Prizes can be redeemed by bank transfer, Zelle or crypto at no stated rate, but deposits can't be withdrawn and a balance must be "fully wagered" first. Its terms provide the service "AS IS" with no authenticity warranty. Rolex FanBoy ($3,743.92) gives a Rolex 25.65% of the time; the rest is a travel pouch, an Oyster link or dealer display gloves. Its live site blocks automated reads, so the box figures here come from September and October 2025 snapshots of its own pages.

=5. IcyBox — 7/12

IcyBox (InfiniteEdge Inc., Delaware) launched in April 2026 as a watch app and added handbags on 1 October 2026. Boxes run from $50 to $1,000 and are bought only in its iOS and Android apps, which show the chance and value range of each tier before you buy.

An item you don't ship within 7 days is sold back automatically at 90%; the rate for a buyback you ask for is not disclosed. Sell-back money can be withdrawn, money you add cannot. Its terms disclaim any authenticity warranty and let it pull a box "even before all Assets ... are distributed (including any grail ...)". Orders take 15–20 business days to ship, with insurance on items of $500 or more.

=5. PackDraw — 7/12

PackDraw (Packdraw Limited, Cyprus, and PackDraw US, LLC; live since 2022) sells packs of watches, designer bags and cars alongside cards, with user-made "community" packs listed next to official ones. Odds and ticket ranges are published per item, and it states the plainest exit in the field: an item can be taken at "100% of its value" as site balance or crypto.

It ships to the US and Canada only, items must be worth $15 or more to ship, and shipping takes 2–3 weeks. Its homepage claims "$500M+ Paid out to players" against "7K+ Items shipped worldwide". In the official Miami Vice pack ($1,353.57), 97.2% of openings are worth less than the price.

=5. Royale — 7/12

Royale (Elite Web Services LTD, Limassol, Cyprus) sells watch, bag, jewelry and designer boxes from $78 to about $2,327, alongside battles, an upgrader and coinflip. Every box lists its contents and odds, and crypto withdrawal is stated at item value less 0.5%. It names no authenticator (StockX appears as a supplier) and lists no excluded states.

Its luxury-named boxes are mostly filler: Luxury Luggage ($950) gives a $0.01 "Fragile Sticker" 80% of the time, and Hublot ($1,500) gives an unwithdrawable "Fake Hublot" 37.5% of the time.

=9. Jemlit — 6/12

Jemlit (JLT Digital Media LTD, Nicosia, Cyprus; on archive.org since 2022) has designer bag, jewelry, Rolex and Cartier boxes, with per-item chances behind a "Show chances" button and a documented provably fair scheme. It verifies age and identity before you can open a box.

Sell-back is stated, at item value less 0–30%, but pays site credit only, never cash, and inactive accounts lose their credit. Its boxes return the least in our checks: about 70–80% of the box price in listed value. Just Rolex ($679.98) gives a Rolex 4.2% of the time.

=9. Lootbox.com — 6/12

Lootbox.com (LOOTBOX LTD, Limassol, Cyprus) lists dozens of brand-named boxes, among them Hermès, Rolex, Cartier, Van Cleef, Goyard and Loro Piana, from about $134 to $6,715, with per-item odds and a documented provably fair scheme. Sell-back is at "the value of the Product" in credits, or in crypto less a fee it doesn't publish.

Its terms say prizes are worth at least the box price, but its own listings include items valued far below it: Timeless Rolex ($862.36) gives a Rolex 1% of the time and a Casio or G-Shock 60% of the time. It makes no authenticity claim.

=11. HypeDrop — 5/12

HypeDrop has run since 2018–19, paused deposits in June 2024 and reopened that August. It is web-only, with Rolex, designer-bag and jewelry boxes; per-item odds sit above every product on a box page, and draws use a server-seed and client-seed scheme. Its own blog names the operator as Plutonomy Limited of Belize, "tied to CSGORoll".

It publishes no floor and doesn't say how values are set beyond "a team working daily". Sell-back pays site balance or crypto (2% fee) at no stated rate, capped at the value you obtained the item for. Its ID check asks for a government ID and a live selfie.

=11. RillaBox — 5/12

RillaBox (TechNexus Ltd, Limassol, Cyprus; on archive.org since 2023) has single-brand boxes from Hermès Birkin to Patek Philippe, from about $7 to $5,000, next to Crash, Plinko and battles. Odds are shown on every box.

Its exit is the hardest to read: sell-back rates appear only after login, unshipped items can be resold automatically after 24 hours, the homepage promises "instant cash" while the terms say "the Site does not allow withdrawals", and crypto withdrawal requires wagering first. Several luxury boxes pay out RillaBox's own vouchers; Rolex Deluxe gives a $200 voucher 26% of the time.

=13. Arena Club (Time Box) — 4/12

Arena Club (Arenaclub.com, Inc., Los Angeles), a trading-card platform, sells one-watch Time Box drops from $50 to $10,000, with Rolex, Cartier, Breitling and others, still labelled Beta. Its help centre says each watch's odds are on the box page, and it offers a 30-day spending limit with a 24-hour cooling-off period before a raise takes effect.

It publishes no floor or valuation method. Watches are inspected by its own team but sold "as-is" with no warranty and no returns. Buyback is an instant offer within 24 hours at no stated rate, and the wallet withdraws to a bank through Stripe.

=13. Courtyard ($500 Watch Box) — 4/12

Courtyard (Courtyard, Inc., Delaware), a graded-card platform, now sells a $500 Watch Box: Royal Pops, MoonSwatch and Tudor, with a Rolex Air-King chase. Its App Store listing promises "real-time odds", but the watch-box figures weren't readable on its pages, and values are "determined solely by Courtyard" from a proprietary algorithm on recent sales.

Its terms, last updated December 2024, never mention watches and disclaim any authenticity warranty. Sell-back is a percentage of fair market value at no stated rate, paid into a USDC balance you can withdraw to a bank, PayPal or Venmo after KYC.

Not ranked

Luxury Lana

A Vancouver pre-owned boutique, "selling luxury goods since 2010", whose four Luxury Mystery Boxes ($350 to $1,550 and up) are hand-picked rather than drawn. It says each box holds more than twice its price in value and that items are authentic preloved or vintage pieces checked by its own team. All sales are final, no company name is given (the contact is a Gmail address), and US shipping is $45. With no draw, the six questions don't fit, so it isn't scored.

What the boxes actually pay out

Where a site publishes every item's odds and listed value, you can work out what one opening returns on average. We did, for two to six luxury boxes per site. These are our calculations, not the sites' figures, and they use each site's own listed values, not resale prices:

  • Cases.gg: about 89.5% of the box price, in line with its stated 10% house edge.
  • Metadraw: 87–90% (Montres Rolex 89.6%, Louis Vuitton 87.2%).
  • Lootbox.com: 88% on all five boxes checked.
  • PackDraw: 88% on Miami Vice.
  • Royale: 87–88% on all five boxes checked.
  • MrLoot: 83–87% on five of six boxes, 91% on its Rolex box.
  • Jemlit: about 70–80%.

StockX works differently: every item in its $300 Accessories box is valued by StockX at $300 or more, with a stated average of $361, but selling one back returns 75–90% of that in StockX Credit. On most sites a sell-back pays at or below listed value, so cashing out an average opening returns less again.

The house on the box is rarely the house in it. Timeless Rolex on Lootbox.com gives a Rolex 1% of the time; Just Rolex on Jemlit 4.2%; Montres Rolex on Metadraw 5.64%; Rolex FanBoy on Cases.gg 25.65%. Royale's Luxury Luggage gives a $0.01 sticker 80% of the time.

Before you open one

  • Check your state. Cases.gg, MrLoot and Metadraw list excluded states; most others list none, which doesn't mean every state is allowed.
  • Know what can leave the site. On IcyBox and Cases.gg, money you add can't be withdrawn; Jemlit pays credit only; StockX pays StockX Credit.
  • Expect ID checks before you withdraw. HypeDrop asks for a government ID and a live selfie; MrLoot and Lootbox.com check ID above set amounts; Jemlit checks before you can open a box.
  • Watch the clock. IcyBox sells an unshipped item back at 90% after 7 days, RillaBox can resell after 24 hours, and StockX's roughly five-minute window defaults to charging you and shipping.
  • Read the authenticity fine print. No site names an independent authenticator, and IcyBox, Courtyard, Arena Club and Cases.gg disclaim authenticity or warranties in their own terms.
  • Set a limit. Every site is 18+ where it states an age. Arena Club offers a built-in 30-day spending limit with a 24-hour cooling-off period; elsewhere, set your own.

Closed, not live or not luxury

Checked and left out: DrakeMall and MysteryBrand (closed, domains parked), MysteryDrop (a password-gated trading-card preview), Luxedrop and Kingdrop (parked domains), HEAT (storefront behind a password), TroveDrops (a free beta with no real prizes), Beezie's Luxury Drop (a one-off drop of 500 boxes with no item odds shown), Lootie (streetwear), Clash.gg (game skins), REVL and HapaBox (no luxury boxes), and Rips by Triumph (trading cards). Unbox.gg, GOATbox.gg and BoxMadness block automated reads, and their archived pages weren't enough to verify them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best luxury mystery box site for 2027?

On our transparency ranking, re:COVET (our sister project, not yet open) and StockX Mystery Boxes tie for first on 10 of 12. Among live sites, StockX is the only one with a published floor, a stated valuation method and its own authentication, though only its $300 Accessories box is designer-led. Metadraw (9) and MrLoot (8) follow; Cases.gg, IcyBox, PackDraw and Royale tie on 7.

Are luxury mystery boxes worth it?

Not as a way to get more back than you pay. On the seven sites where we worked it out from published odds, an opening returns less than the box price in listed value on average: about 83–91% on most and 70–80% on Jemlit. StockX's own figures put its $300 box's average at $361, but its sell-back pays 75–90% in StockX Credit. Most openings of a brand-named box are other goods or site credit.

Do luxury mystery box sites publish their odds?

Twelve of the 13 live sites ranked say they show per-item or per-tier odds before you buy; Courtyard advertises "real-time odds" without saying where. Among live sites, only StockX, Metadraw, MrLoot and IcyBox state a value range or floor for a box, and only Cases.gg states its house edge (10%). re:COVET's Terms commit it to publishing tier probabilities and a floor for every box.

Can you sell back an item from a luxury mystery box?

On all 13 live sites ranked, yes, but the rate and what you're paid in vary. PackDraw states 100% of an item's value as balance or crypto, Royale item value less 0.5% in crypto, IcyBox 90% automatically after 7 days, StockX 75–90% in StockX Credit, and Jemlit up to 30% less in site credit. Most others state no rate.

Which luxury mystery box sites are US companies?

StockX (Detroit), IcyBox (Delaware), Courtyard (Delaware) and Arena Club (Los Angeles). Eight of the other ten are Cyprus companies, and HypeDrop's blog names a Belize company. None of the 14 states a gaming licence. re:COVET, not yet open, is Curated Drops, LLC, of Delaware.

Final verdict

On paper, our sister project re:COVET ties StockX for the most disclosed, but it hasn't opened, it publishes no sell-back rate, and like most boxes here it prices a box above the average fair market value inside. Among live sites, StockX's $300 Accessories box tells you the most: floor, average, ceiling, odds, how items are valued and who verifies them. Its exit pays StockX Credit. Among sites built around luxury boxes, Metadraw publishes the most, and PackDraw and Royale state the plainest exits. Whichever you choose, read the box's own odds first. The name on the box is rarely what most openings get.
