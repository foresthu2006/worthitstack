GoHighLevel vs Keap: Which CRM Wins in 2026?

In short: Pick GoHighLevel if you run an agency — its sub-account and SaaS model has no rival — or want flat unlimited-contact billing starting at $97/month. Pick Keap if you're a small business with a modest list where the contact tax doesn't bite, $129–$229/month. Agencies shouldn't compare these — Keap has no agency model.

On paper they're the same pitch: one subscription that replaces your CRM, email tool, funnel builder, and appointment scheduler. In practice they're built for opposite buyers. Keap (the company formerly known as Infusionsoft) is the small-business automation veteran — the deepest campaign builder in this price class, billing you per contact. GoHighLevel is the agency disruptor — flat pricing with unlimited contacts and a sub-account model that lets agencies run dozens of clients on one bill.

The entire comparison reduces to one structural difference: Keap's bill grows with your list; GoHighLevel's doesn't. Everything else — features, moats, catches — flows from that. Here's the contact-tax math, the feature comparison, and the honest verdict.

The quick verdict

Pick GoHighLevel if you run an agency (the sub-account + SaaS model has no rival), want flat unlimited-contact billing, or need funnels, websites, and a phone system in one bill. Entry: $97/month flat, unlimited contacts.

Pick Keap if you're a single small business that lives inside automation — its campaign builder is the deepest in the class — and your list is modest enough that the contact tax doesn't bite. Entry: roughly $129–$229/month at published 2026 rates, contact-limited.

The honest split: agencies shouldn't be comparing these two at all — Keap has no agency model. And solo operators should price year one with their actual list size, not the sticker price.

The contact-tax math that decides it

This is the section that matters most, so let's run real scenarios. Assume one user, one business, no add-ons.

Scenario A: 2,000 contacts, growing slowly. GoHighLevel Starter: $97/mo + ~$30/mo usage = ~$127/mo real bill, and the list can triple without the bill moving. Keap: a published Pro-tier rate (roughly $129–$229/mo) covers 1,500 contacts — you're already over the base allowance, so contact-tier step-ups apply. At this size the two are roughly comparable; Keap's deeper automation may be worth the premium.

Scenario B: 10,000 contacts. GoHighLevel Starter: still $97/mo + usage (~$127/mo) — the bill doesn't care about your list. Keap: you're several contact tiers past the 1,500–2,500 base allowance, and published guidance puts you in the $200+/mo zone on Pro-tier before extra users. Expect to pay at least 2–3× the GoHighLevel Starter rate for equivalent access — and every 5,000 contacts you add reopens the pricing conversation.

Scenario C: agency, 12 clients. GoHighLevel Agency Pro: $497/mo, unlimited sub-accounts — ~$41/mo per client, plus SaaS mode lets you rebill clients at your own price and keep the margin. Keap: no sub-account model exists — each client needs its own Keap account at full price. Twelve Pro-tier accounts at ~$129–$229/mo each = $1,548–$2,748/mo. This isn't a comparison; it's a category error. Agencies should not be choosing Keap.

The honest framing: Keap's contact tax is fair pricing for what it is — deep automation has a per-record cost, and Infusionsoft always billed this way. But it means Keap gets more expensive the more successful you get, while GoHighLevel's bill is decoupled from your growth. Price your year-one and year-three bills, not the sticker.

Keap's automation moat

This is where Keap earns its premium, and it's real. The campaign builder is the direct descendant of Infusionsoft's — twenty-plus years of small-business automation DNA, refined into a visual builder that handles branching logic, lead scoring, marketing analytics, promo codes, and checkout upsells (Max tier) that GoHighLevel's workflow builder matches in breadth but not in depth.

Keap Pay is the underrated piece: invoicing, one-click payments, and recurring billing built into the CRM at 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction with no monthly fee. For service businesses and info-product sellers who live in order forms and payment plans, that's one fewer integration to babysit — Stripe bolted onto GoHighLevel works, but it isn't native.

And the small-business ergonomics matter: dedicated phone line on Pro, 100+ custom fields, prebuilt campaign templates loaded with best-practice steps, appointments synced to Google Calendar. Keap is optimized for the solo operator who wants sophisticated automation without a RevOps hire.

GoHighLevel's counterpunch

GoHighLevel doesn't try to out-automate Keap — it outflanks it. Flat unlimited-contact billing means your bill is decoupled from your growth (see the contact-tax math above). The funnel and website builder plus memberships mean Keap's landing-pages-only approach looks thin. The LC phone/SMS system with rebilling turns communication from a cost center into a margin line for agencies.

But the real counterpunch is SaaS mode: Agency Pro at $497/month lets you resell GoHighLevel itself to clients at whatever price you set, white-labeled. Run 20 clients at $97/month each and the platform pays for itself four times over before you touch a service retainer. Keap has no answer to this — its partner program pays referral-style, not resale-style.

Entry price seals it for the curious: $97/month flat to start, versus Keap's Pro-tier entry at roughly 1.3–2.3× that, contact-limited.

Who should pick GoHighLevel, and who should pick Keap?

Pick GoHighLevel if you run an agency — sub-accounts and the SaaS model are the whole ballgame — or want flat $97/month billing decoupled from contact count. Pick Keap if you're a small business living inside automation with a modest list, at $129–$229/month. Agencies shouldn't compare: Keap has no agency model.

Pick GoHighLevel if: you run an agency or plan to (sub-accounts + SaaS mode are the whole ballgame); your list is growing fast and you want the bill decoupled from it; you want funnels, websites, phone, and memberships on one invoice; your budget starts at $97/month.

Pick Keap if: you're a single small business with a modest list (under ~5,000 contacts) that lives inside automation — deep branching, lead scoring, upsell funnels, payment plans; you value the deepest campaign builder in the class over the broadest feature set; and you're comfortable with a bill that grows as your list does.

Skip Keap if: you manage client accounts (no sub-accounts — full price per client), your list is already past 10,000 contacts (do the contact-tax math first), or you need real website/funnel building rather than landing pages.

Skip GoHighLevel if: you need the deepest automation branching available and nothing else matters — Keap's campaign builder still wins that fight; or you want a tool with a gentler learning curve (GoHighLevel's 2–4 week ramp is real).

Frequently asked questions

Is GoHighLevel cheaper than Keap?

Yes at the entry tier, and the gap grows with your list. GoHighLevel Starter is $97/month flat with unlimited contacts. Keap's 2026 Pro-tier rates run roughly $129–$229/month by billing cycle and contact tier, with only 1,500–2,500 contacts at the base level. Because Keap bills per contact, its bill climbs while GoHighLevel's stays flat.

How does Keap's contact-based pricing work?

Your plan includes a contact allowance — 1,500 contacts on Pro and 2,500 on Max — and the price steps up as your list grows past those tiers. Extra users cost roughly $29–$30/month each, and Keap Pay adds 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction with no monthly fee. There's a 14-day trial and no free plan.

Can agencies run multiple clients on one Keap account?

No — Keap has no sub-account model; each client needs its own account at full price. GoHighLevel's Agency Pro ($497/month, $4,970/year) includes unlimited sub-accounts plus SaaS mode for reselling to clients with white-label branding. For agencies managing more than two or three clients, this is the single biggest structural difference.

What is Keap Max Classic?

Max Classic is Infusionsoft renamed — Keap's original flagship — still sold on custom-quote pricing for legacy customers. If you're an old Infusionsoft user, you're likely on it; new buyers are generally steered to the current Pro and Max tiers. Expect a sales-led quote process rather than self-serve checkout on Max Classic.

Which has the better automation builder?

Keap, for automation depth: its visual campaign builder is the descendant of Infusionsoft's, with lead scoring, marketing analytics, promo codes, and checkout upsells on Max — the deepest branching logic of the two. GoHighLevel's workflow builder is strong and covers the gap in breadth (funnels, websites, LC phone/SMS, memberships, SaaS mode), but pure automation sophistication still belongs to Keap.

Is GoHighLevel's $97/month really the whole bill?

No. $97 Starter is the floor, not the ceiling: expect $20–$150/month in usage fees (calls, SMS, email, AI) on top, and the AI Employee add-on is $50/month per sub-account on Starter. Keap has the same problem — the sticker price isn't the whole bill, because contacts grow the bill. Do the year-one math first.

Final verdict

Worth it for agencies and growth-stage businesses: GoHighLevel. $97/month flat with unlimited contacts, sub-accounts, SaaS mode, funnels, and a rebillable phone system — the bill is decoupled from your growth, and Agency Pro at $497/month is the cheapest per-client stack in the category. Just budget the real bill ($20–$150/mo usage on top) and the 2–4 week learning curve.

Worth it for automation-first solo businesses: Keap. The deepest campaign builder in the class, native Keap Pay, lead scoring and upsell funnels on Max — if your list is modest and your automation is sophisticated, the contact tax is a fair price for the depth. Just price it at your year-three list size, not today's.

Skip the comparison entirely if you're an agency eyeing Keap — one business per account at full price per client is a non-starter next to unlimited sub-accounts. And skip GoHighLevel if the deepest automation branching is the only thing you care about; Keap still wins that one fight.
