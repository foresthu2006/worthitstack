Why do people switch from HeyGen?

Reviewers cite five reasons: premium-avatar credit burn (about 3–7x the $29 headline for the best-looking output), billing anxiety (credits don't transfer or refund on cancel), weak native voiceover (serious users add an ElevenLabs subscription), downgrade friction (no self-service downgrade — reviewers report emailing support), and team pricing ($149/month plus $20 per extra seat):

  • Credit burn on premium avatars. One finished minute costs roughly 7 credits on Avatar III, 16–20 on Avatar IV, and around 48 on Avatar V. Creator's 600 credits sound generous until you do the premium math — the best-looking output costs 3–7x what the headline implies.
  • Billing anxiety. Credits meter everything, and credits don't transfer or get refunded when you cancel. Users who just want to know "what does a minute cost" find meter-based billing stressful compared to flat minute plans.
  • Weak native voiceover. Serious users pair HeyGen with ElevenLabs for voice quality, which means a second subscription and a second workflow for what should be one tool.
  • Downgrade friction. Reviewers report that downgrading requires emailing support rather than a self-service click in billing settings — never a good sign when the bill is the complaint.
  • The team price. Business is $149/month plus $20 per extra seat. Teams that need five people rendering regularly do the math and flinch.

The 7 alternatives, reviewed

1. Synthesia — the calmest alternative

Synthesia is the enterprise default and HeyGen's closest like-for-like rival: 160+ languages, a genuinely free Basic tier (10 minutes per month, watermarked), and the deepest compliance stack in the category (SOC 2 Type II, GDPR with EU data residency). Starter is $29/mo (annual billing available) with predictable minute-based billing — the exact opposite of credit anxiety. For training and enablement content, it's the safer pick. Try Synthesia free

The catch: its avatars read as synthetic next to HeyGen's Avatar IV/V — professional but stiff. If realism is the deliverable, Synthesia doesn't win that fight. See our full HeyGen vs Synthesia breakdown.

2. Colossyan — the L&D pick

Colossyan is purpose-built for workplace learning: avatars plus quizzes, branching scenarios, and SCORM export from the Professional plan ($59/mo billed annually) — features HeyGen's plans don't prioritize. The free Starter tier (20 minutes per month, watermarked) is the most generous free plan among like-for-like tools.

The catch: avatar realism lags HeyGen by a visible margin, and outside training workflows it's less versatile than the generalists.

3. D-ID — the cheapest entry point

D-ID's Lite plan at $4.70/mo billed annually is the lowest credible sticker in the category, and its API-first architecture makes it the pick for developers building talking-head features into products. The 14-day trial lets you test before paying. Its avatars are talking photos rather than full-body presenters — a different product, but enough for simple explainers.

The catch: not a like-for-like HeyGen replacement. No full-body avatars, limited scene control, and reviews note it feels limited for higher-production work.

4. Elai — the budget converter

Elai ingests what you already have — PDFs, decks, URLs — and turns them into narrated avatar videos. Creator is $29/mo ($23/mo billed annually), undercutting HeyGen's Creator while covering the document-to-video workflow teams actually use. The free tier covers 1 minute per month.

The catch: smaller avatar library, less polish on the enterprise side, and a thinner integration list than the category leaders.

5. VEED — the editor that also does avatars

VEED is a real browser-based video editor first, with AI avatars as one feature among auto-subtitles, translations, and templates — at ~$12/mo billed annually it's the practical pick if you want an editing timeline as much as an avatar. The free tier is watermarked but genuinely usable.

The catch: its avatars are a side feature, not the product. If the avatar is the whole deliverable, the specialists render better.

6. Captions — the short-form ad pick

Captions (Max plan $24.99/mo) built its reputation on short-form: its Mirage avatar models are tuned for the fast, punchy clips that run as social ads — directly competitive with HeyGen's own avatar-ad workflows. If your "video production" is 30-second verticals, this is a better fit than a generalist platform.

The catch: it's a short-form tool. Long explainers, multilingual training modules, and LMS publishing are outside its lane.
