HeyGen and Synthesia are the two names that come up whenever someone asks about AI avatar videos, and on paper they look interchangeable: type a script, pick a digital presenter, get a video. The sticker prices even match — both start at $29/month. But the similarity ends at the checkout page. Synthesia is built for corporate training and compliance: SCORM export for LMS platforms, enterprise security certifications, and a pricing model metered in video minutes. HeyGen is built for marketing and creators: more realistic avatars, UGC-style ad workflows, voice cloning, and a credit system that rewards short-form content.

The trap in this comparison: the $29 headline plans buy you completely different amounts of actual video. This is the one comparison where you must do the per-minute math before picking. Here's the full breakdown.

The per-minute math that decides it

This is the section most comparisons skip, and it's the whole decision. Synthesia's minute math is exact: the vendor's pricing page maps 1,250 credits to roughly 12 finished minutes per month (about 1.7 credits per second of output) — enough for roughly four short training modules. If you publish a weekly 5-minute video, you've consumed your Starter allowance by week two and you're looking at the Pro plan ($89/month) for ~60 minutes (6,000 credits).

HeyGen's math is looser by design: the Creator plan's ~15 credits convert to roughly ~15 minutes of avatar video per month (1 credit ≈ 2 minutes of video avatar, ≈ 5 minutes of photo avatar), with standard avatar videos described as unlimited on paid plans in 2026 reviews. The catch is the add-on economy: extra credit packs run $15 for 300 credits, priority processing is $15/month (standard queues reportedly take 10–30 minutes at peak on Creator), and 2026 reviews estimate a realistic power user pays ~$59/month, not $29, once add-ons are included.

The honest comparison: if your content is short-form marketing clips, HeyGen's model is cheaper per finished video. If your content is 5–10 minute training modules on a fixed schedule, Synthesia's per-minute metering is at least predictable — you know exactly when you'll hit the ceiling, and the Creator plan's 30 minutes/month covers a weekly publishing cadence.

Avatar realism vs corporate polish

HeyGen's edge — and why marketers pick it. Across 2026 creator reviews, HeyGen's avatar realism consistently rates as the best in its price range: more natural facial motion, better lip-sync on casual speech, and a photo-avatar mode that turns a single image into a talking presenter. That realism is what makes UGC-style ads work — a stiff corporate avatar kills a TikTok ad in two seconds. HeyGen also leans into creator workflows: templates built for ads and social, faster iteration on short clips, and a voice library tuned for marketing energy rather than training neutrality.

Synthesia's edge — and why enterprises pick it. Synthesia's Express-2 avatar engine produces full-body presenters with gestures and micro-expressions, and the output reads "professional training module," not "AI video." For compliance-heavy organizations, the real differentiator isn't the avatar at all — it's the compliance stack (SOC 2 Type II, ISO certifications, EU data residency) and LMS integration via SCORM export. When your videos feed into a corporate learning platform with audit requirements, that's a purchase requirement, not a nice-to-have. Reviewers also consistently note Synthesia's dubbing: translate one training video into 30+ languages with lip-sync, a genuine cost-killer for multinational onboarding.
