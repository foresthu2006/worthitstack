Two things to note before anything else. First, the credit allowance is the plan, not the price — two teams on Starter can have wildly different experiences depending on whether they need 8 minutes or 14 minutes a month. Second, avatar quality does not scale with price the way you'd hope. The credit meter that defines your bill

This is the section the pricing page should have. Synthesia's pricing page maps 1,250 credits to roughly 12 minutes of finished video and 6,000 credits to roughly 60 minutes — about 1.7 credits per second of generated video.

Two properties of the meter matter more than the numbers:

  • No rollover. Unused credits expire at the end of each billing cycle. A quiet month doesn't bank time for a busy one — the meter resets whether you used it or not.
  • Every regeneration costs. Change the script, regenerate the scene, and the meter runs again on the new output. Iterative editing — the normal way humans make video — is the fastest way to burn through a month's allowance. Storyboard first, generate once, then revise sparingly.

The math that matters: at Starter's $29/month price for ~12 minutes, you're paying roughly $2.40 per finished minute — still vastly cheaper than filming, and competitive for talking-head content. But the moment your roadmap needs 12–15 minutes a month, Pro at $89/month is the honest tier, and the price jumps 3× for a 5× minute allowance. There is no gentle middle step. Map your real monthly output before you pick.

The free plan's real limits

Basic is a trial, not a tier: a handful of minutes to test avatar quality, voices, and the editor on your own script, with no credit card required. It's genuinely useful for one decision — "does this avatar style work for my content?" — and genuinely useless for anything else. The limits are the minute count and feature gates on personal avatars and brand assets. If the trial convinces you, expect to land on Starter (solo/small teams) or Pro (teams with a real content calendar).

What it actually does

Type a script, pick an avatar, arrange scenes in the editor, export. The 2026 feature set, in order of what actually matters:

  • 230+ stock avatars — the largest library in the category, spanning ages, ethnicities, and presentation styles, including the newer-generation models with noticeably better lip-sync.
  • Personal avatars — built from a short webcam recording of you reading a script, with identity verification and consent required. Quality tiers up on Pro; this is the feature for founders and course creators who want "them, but scalable."
  • 140+ languages and voices — one script, dozens of localized versions. This is where Synthesia's enterprise training use case shines: update the English source video and regenerate every language in an afternoon.
  • 65+ video templates — training, onboarding, sales, marketing, and internal comms layouts that actually look designed, not defaulted.
  • Screen/page recorder — capture your screen alongside the avatar for walkthrough and tutorial content (Pro+).
  • Brand kit — logos, fonts, colors, intros/outros baked into every render so output looks like your company, not a template.
  • API + integrations — Enterprise-tier programmatic generation for teams rendering video at software scale; integrations across the usual LMS and collaboration tools.

The three catches

1. The credit meter makes Starter a ~12-minute plan

Covered above, but it belongs here because it's the #1 billing surprise: $29/month sounds like a subscription to a video platform; it's actually a subscription to ~12 minutes of output with no rollover and no cheap middle step. Teams that underestimate their monthly minutes hit the wall in week three and face a 3× jump to Pro. Count first.

2. Realism is very good, not invisible

The newest avatars are convincing at a glance — good lip-sync, natural cadence, none of the robotic stiffness of 2023-era models. But look for it and you'll spot slightly mechanical mouth movement and limited hand and body language; it's a talking head, not a person. For training modules it's a non-issue. For high-trust marketing or sales content where the viewer might look twice, HeyGen's latest models edge Synthesia on realism — and either way, test with your own script before committing.

3. Personal avatars take real setup, and iteration is metered

A personal avatar needs a clean webcam recording session, identity verification, and processing time — it's not instant, and the best quality requires Creator. More importantly, every script revision re-runs the meter, so the "iterate freely" workflow of normal video editing becomes "iterate expensively." The teams that love Synthesia storyboard and lock scripts before generating; the teams that hate it discover the meter mid-revision.

Who should buy Synthesia?

Buy it if: you run L&D, onboarding, or internal communications where video volume is high and reshoots are expensive — multilingual training libraries are Synthesia's home turf, and the per-minute economics crush filming. Also strong for course creators and agencies that need a consistent on-brand presenter across dozens of videos.

Skip it if: your monthly output lives in the awkward 12–20 minute band (Starter is too small, Pro is a 3× jump), you need the absolute best avatar realism for marketing (compare HeyGen first), or you're a solo creator on a budget (D-ID's entry tier is a fraction of the price — see the alternatives).
