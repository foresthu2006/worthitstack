Do unused Synthesia credits roll over?

Why do people switch from Synthesia?

The four reasons that recur: the credit meter (about 1.7 credits per second of standard video; unused credits don't roll over), SCORM export gated to enterprise pricing, avatars that read as synthetic next to newer, more expressive rivals, and per-minute math that punishes casual creators (every re-render costs you):

  • The credit meter. Synthesia meters output in credits (about 1.7 credits per second of standard video — 1,250 credits ≈ 12 minutes, 6,000 ≈ 60 minutes), and unused credits don't roll over. A minute-based bill punishes iteration — every re-render costs you.
  • SCORM is enterprise-gated. Training teams publishing to an LMS need SCORM export, and Synthesia reserves it for custom-priced Enterprise plans. Competitors ship it on standard paid tiers.
  • The avatars read as synthetic. Synthesia's presenters are professional but stiff — newer rivals render more expressive, more human-looking avatars that perform better in marketing content.
  • The per-minute math. Starter's 10 minutes per month is fine for steady training output, but casual creators pay a premium per finished minute compared to cheaper tools for simple clips.

The 7 alternatives, reviewed

1. HeyGen — the best overall alternative

HeyGen is the closest like-for-like rival and the one most reviewers name first: more realistic, more expressive avatars than Synthesia, 175+ languages, and video translation on paid plans. Creator runs $29/mo ($24/mo billed annually), with a free tier covering 3 watermarked videos per month. For marketing content, ads, and creator workflows, it's the stronger pick.

The catch: it doesn't match Synthesia's enterprise compliance stack, and for structured training content the difference in realism matters less than Synthesia's governance features. See our full HeyGen vs Synthesia breakdown.

2. Colossyan — the L&D pick

Colossyan is purpose-built for workplace learning: avatars plus quizzes, branching scenarios, and SCORM export from the Professional plan ($59/mo billed annually) — features Synthesia reserves for enterprise contracts. The free Starter tier (20 minutes per month, watermarked) is the most generous free plan among like-for-like tools.

The catch: avatar realism lags HeyGen, and outside training workflows it's less versatile than the generalists.

3. D-ID — the cheapest entry point

D-ID's Lite plan at $4.70/mo billed annually is the lowest credible sticker in the category, and its API-first architecture makes it the pick for developers building talking-head features into products. The 14-day trial lets you test before paying. Its avatars are talking photos rather than full-body presenters — a different product, but enough for simple explainers.

The catch: not a like-for-like Synthesia replacement. No full-body avatars, limited scene control, and reviews note it feels limited for higher-production work.

4. Elai — the budget converter

Elai ingests what you already have — PDFs, decks, URLs — and turns them into narrated avatar videos. Creator is $29/mo ($23/mo billed annually), undercutting Synthesia's Starter while covering the document-to-video workflow teams actually use. The free tier covers 1 minute per month.

The catch: smaller avatar library, less polish on the enterprise side, and a thinner integration list than the category leaders.

5. VEED — the editor that also does avatars

VEED is a real browser-based video editor first, with AI avatars as one feature among auto-subtitles, translations, and templates — at ~$12/mo billed annually it's the practical pick if you want an editing timeline as much as an avatar. The free tier is watermarked but genuinely usable.

The catch: its avatars are a side feature, not the product. If the avatar is the whole deliverable, the specialists render better.

6. Captions — the short-form ad pick

Captions (Max plan $24.99/mo) built its reputation on short-form: its Mirage avatar models are tuned for the fast, punchy clips that run as social ads. If your "video production" is 30-second verticals, this is a better fit than a training-first platform.

The catch: it's a short-form tool. Long explainers, multilingual training modules, and LMS publishing are outside its lane.

7. Synthesia itself — don't switch without reading this

Honest take: if you're an enterprise learning team, half the people researching "Synthesia alternatives" should just stay. The compliance stack (SOC 2 Type II, GDPR with EU data residency), 160+ languages, AI dubbing, and a free Basic tier for testing add up to the safest corporate pick — and Starter at $29/mo (annual billing available) is fairly priced for training output.
