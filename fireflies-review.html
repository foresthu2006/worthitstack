The AI credit pool that changes the bill

This is the section the pricing page should have. Fireflies runs on AI credits: meeting summaries, action-item detection, AskFred queries, and meeting highlights all consume credits. Each plan includes a one-time shared workspace pool — 20 credits on Free and Pro, 30 on Business, 50 on Enterprise. One-time, shared across your whole workspace, not a monthly per-user refill.

What that means in practice: a light user (a few calls a week, skims the summaries) may never notice the pool. A heavy user — a sales rep who asks AskFred five questions after every call, runs AI Skills automations on every meeting — can burn through the pool and hit a wall where AI features pause until more credits are purchased. Add-on bundles run roughly $0.06–$0.10 per credit depending on bundle size and auto-renew monthly unless paused.

The math: on Pro's $10/seat annual headline, a heavy team should budget a credit bundle the way they'd budget a utility bill. It's not a dealbreaker — the credits are cheap per unit — but it is the thing that turns "unlimited transcription and AI summaries" into "unlimited transcription, metered AI." Know which kind of user you are before you pick the tier. Business's larger pool plus unlimited storage is the tier for teams that live in AskFred.

The free plan's real limits

The free plan is unusually honest for the category: unlimited transcription in 100+ languages, meeting search, real-time notes, and AskFred access, with no credit card required. The limits are storage (~800 minutes per seat — a busy week of calls fills it), no video recording, limited AI summaries, and the 20-credit AI pool cap. Treat it as a real trial of the transcription engine, not a permanent tier — if the quality holds up on your calls, the $10/seat Pro annual is the natural next step.

What it actually does

Fireflies joins your calls as a bot (or captures bot-free via the Chrome extension for Google Meet and the desktop app), then turns the recording into a searchable, summarized, action-itemed record. The 2026 feature set, in order of what actually matters:

  • AskFred — the differentiator. A chatbot that answers questions across your entire meeting history (plus email, Slack, and CRM data via connectors), generates follow-up content, and writes reports from past conversations. This is the feature people stay for.
  • AI Skills — 200+ post-meeting automations across sales, recruiting, marketing, and ops that run per meeting or on a schedule across multiple meetings.
  • Email + Slack connectors — Auto Draft writes replies using both the email thread and what was said on your calls; Follow-ups catches unanswered threads and drafts the nudge. Every draft waits for your review before sending.
  • Conversation intelligence — talk-to-listen ratios, sentiment analysis, topic tracking, custom vocabulary (Business+). The sales-coaching layer that justifies the Business tier for revenue teams.
  • 100+ integrations — HubSpot, Salesforce, Jira, Asana, Slack, Notion, Zapier, and more. CRM automation (logging calls, updating records) is built for sales workflows, not bolted on.
  • 100+ languages with automatic language detection — among the widest coverage in the category (Otter's core support covers far fewer).
  • Compliance — GDPR, SOC 2 Type II, FERPA across the board; HIPAA-BAA reserved for Enterprise.

The three catches

1. The AI credit pool is a one-time, shared wallet

Covered above, but it belongs here too because it's the #1 billing surprise: "unlimited transcription and AI summaries" on Pro is true for transcription and capped by the 20-credit shared pool for the AI layer. Heavy AskFred and AI Skills users should plan on bundles (~$0.06–$0.10/credit) or the Business tier's bigger pool.

2. No live captions — everything happens after the call

Fireflies processes recordings once the meeting ends; transcripts and summaries typically arrive within minutes. If you need on-screen captions during the call — for accessibility, multilingual teams, or anyone who reads along — Otter.ai does real-time properly and Fireflies simply doesn't.

3. Accuracy is very good, not the 99% on the tin

Fireflies claims up to 99% transcription accuracy for English. Independent benchmarks consistently score it a few points lower on clear, native-English audio, and accuracy drops noticeably with strong accents or heavy crosstalk. That gap exists across every AI meeting assistant, so it's not a Fireflies-specific failure — but it is why you test the free plan on your calls, with your audio, before buying seats for a team.
